## QA-03 synthetic resistor evaluation

Policy v2 was selected on the development render grid and frozen before the
held-out grid was run. The rule holds an otherwise-`ok` interpretation if at
least 10% of any one decoded band has a channel value of 250 or greater. This
is a conservative heuristic, not a calibrated probability. Native evidence is
preserved unchanged.

| Metric | v0.1.1 dev | v0.1.2 dev | v0.1.1 held-out | v0.1.2 held-out |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Renders | 1,200 | 1,200 | 512 | 512 |
| Native `ok` | 933 | 933 | 196 | 196 |
| Accepted correct | 338 | 294 | 24 | 15 |
| Accepted incorrect | 58 | 25 | 43 | 23 |
| Rejected correct | 226 | 270 | 38 | 47 |
| Rejected incorrect | 311 | 344 | 91 | 111 |
| Candidate precision | 85.4% | 92.2% | 35.8% | 39.5% |
| Candidate coverage of native `ok` | 42.5% | 34.2% | 34.2% | 19.4% |

Held-out false-acceptance rate among incorrect native-`ok` results decreased
from 43/134 (32.1%) to 23/134 (17.2%). Coverage also decreased. Remaining
false-confident examples include an expected 82 kOhm gray-red-orange-gold
sample interpreted as 8.2 Ohm gray-red-gold-gold. Its bands are internally
consistent and unclipped, so the v0.1.2 clipping rule cannot detect the color
ambiguity. The known 2.2 kOhm glare fixture still yields a native 22.9 kOhm
candidate, but the Core verdict is `ambiguous`; the native result and evidence
remain unchanged. None of these synthetic results is hardware validation or
sufficient evidence to trust automatic resistor identification.
